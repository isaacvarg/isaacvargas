## The Problem

My dad built a spreadsheet to help our family coordinate care for my grandma: who was covering which shifts, what caregivers were owed, and how those costs fit into the household budget. It worked for a while, but with five people editing it and the data getting more complicated, the limits of a shared spreadsheet started to show. There was no record of who had changed what; shift trades happened over text messages and were easy to lose track of; and the money tied to caregiving lived completely separate from the rest of the household's finances.

## The Solution

Con Cariño ("with love" in Spanish) is a self-hosted app built for a single family: a shared household ledger and a care coordination system living side by side. It tracks accounts, transactions, and reconciliation the way a personal finance app would, ties caregiver hours directly into invoices, and lets caregivers and family memebers swap shifts without losing coverage or paperwork.

## Key Technical Decisions & Features

### Single-Tenant 

Most apps default to multi-tenancy even when only one household will ever use them. Con Cariño intentionally skips that complexity: there's no organization model, minimal roles, and no invite system.  Cutting that entire category of permissions logic freed up time to spend on the features the family actually needed: the ledger, the care calendar, and invoicing.

### Invoice Snapshotting

The app features paid caregivers which have parametric pay dates, rates, off-time rates, typical schedule, and more. Caregiver hours accrue from scheduled shifts and roll up into invoices. Rather than computing pay at read time from a caregiver's current rate, each invoice line snapshots the rate and hours as they were when the invoice was generated. 

### Shift Swaps

Coverage is built from recurring weekly, biweekly or set intervals that materialize into individual coverage shifts. In reality, real caregiving schedules changes constantly, from assigned family members trying to swap their weekends to paid caregivers needing to modify their schedule. The swap system lets a caregiver hand off one or more windows to another, optionally offering some of their own time back in return. If the caregiver losing coverage has an app account, they approve the swap themselves; if they're an offline caregiver who never logs in, anyone can approve on their behalf. That distinction kept the system usable for family members who help out but will never touch the app. Additionally, approval for app users also features automated emails.

### Reconciliation and an Audit Trail

Every ledger row can be worked against a bank statement in reconciliation mode, moving through uncleared, cleared, needs review, and reconciled states. Since several family members touch the same accounts, a field-level audit log records what changed, from what, to what, and by whom, across the whole app rather than just the ledger. That log is what makes shared data trustworthy without a single person having to police every edit.

## What I Learned

This project pushed me to think about financial correctness the way a real accounting system would. Transfers are written as two paired rows so both accounts stay honest, and invoices snapshot rate and hours instead of recalculating from current state; small decisions like these are what keep a shared ledger trustworthy when five people are editing it. I also learned the value of deliberately not building for scale: designing for exactly one family removed entire layers of complexity that would have gone unused and let that effort go toward the audit log, reconciliation, and swap-approval flows the family actually needed. It was also nice using TanStack Start and Router to go out of my confort zone of Next.js app router.
